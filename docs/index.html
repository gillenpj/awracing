Statistical models of UK All-Weather racing outcomes

A series of papers built on the Smartform database in R. Source code and pipeline at github.com/gillenpj/awracing.

Papers

  • Paper 1: Replicating Owen (2019) on UK All-Weather Flat handicaps, 2006–2015 — PDF — replicates Owen's conditional-logit win model on a different dataset, All-Weather rather than Turf: coefficient signs largely agree with his, and the fitted model trails the market on both scoring rules
  • Paper 2a: Extended feature set and race-level interactions on UK All-Weather Flat handicaps — PDF — adds an extended feature set and race-level interactions to the win model: the official rating is the strongest new predictor, a course-specific draw bias appears at Kempton and Southwell, and the narrowed loss is not distinguishable from paper 1's
  • Paper 2b: Ranking models for UK All-Weather Flat handicaps — the exploded conditional logit and the place market — PDF — holds the feature set fixed and changes the objective from predicting the winner to predicting the finishing order: a better winner-picker than paper 2a, still behind the market on ranking, and single-bet place returns about half the drag of backing every qualifier
  • Paper 3: Gradient boosted trees for UK All-Weather Flat handicaps — PDF — holds the features and the objective fixed and changes the function class to a gradient-boosted tree ensemble: ties paper 2b on every measure tested, with the market ahead of both on ranking
    • Supplementary: Notes on tree-based methods — background theory the paper's derivation follows, from regression trees through gradient boosting and XGBoost, and its application to horse racing
  • Paper 4: The marginal value of a model over the market — PDF — tests whether the model adds anything to the market price rather than whether it beats it: nothing distinguishable against the settled starting price, a great deal against the pre-race forecast price
  • Paper 5: Sequence encoding of run histories — PDF — replaces the eight hand-built features summarising a horse's own run history with a recurrent encoder that reads the twenty most recent runs themselves: beats the summaries on every ranking measure on the test split, and three models separate cleanly where papers 1–3 could not be told apart, with the market still ahead
    • Supplementary: Notes on neural scorers and sequence encoders — background theory the paper's machinery follows, from neural networks and how they are trained through the Plackett–Luce likelihood under a neural scorer, embeddings, and recurrent sequence encoders
  • Paper 6: What the metrics can and cannot tell us — PDF — asks what the series' own measures can actually support. Return on investment cannot tell one model from the next: one model's return swings 33 points between adjacent sets of races. Owen's two scoring rules can, and they separate the paper-5 encoder where the return could not. Against two benchmarks, every model in the series loses to the settled starting price and beats the overnight forecast price
